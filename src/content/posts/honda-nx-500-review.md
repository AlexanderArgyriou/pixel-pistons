# Honda NX 500: The Adventure Bike That Actually Makes Sense

Let's cut through the marketing: the Honda NX 500 isn't going to win shootouts against the Yamaha Ténéré 700 or KTM 890 Adventure. It's not trying to. This is Honda's answer to a simple question: what if you made an adventure bike for people who actually adventure, not Instagram models posing in Starbucks parking lots?

After 5,000 kilometers across highways, city streets, gravel roads, and the occasional "why did I think this was a good idea?" trail, I have thoughts. Strong ones. Let me tell you about this bike.

## First Impressions: Understated Competence

The NX 500 doesn't announce itself. No dramatic beak, no "LOOK AT ME" graphics, no bolt-on luggage that costs more than a used car. It's... normal. In the best way.

When I picked mine up, the dealer said, "It's not flashy, but you'll appreciate that in six months." He was right. While other adventure bikes scream "I'm going to Morocco!" (narrator: they're going to Whole Foods), the NX 500 just looks like a bike that wants to ride.

The ergonomics hit me immediately: this is a bike designed by people who ride. The seat height (830mm) is reasonable for normal humans. The handlebars are where your hands naturally want to be. The pegs don't require yoga flexibility. It's comfortable in a way that feels... Japanese. Like they measured actual humans, not aspirational adventure riders with 36" inseams.

Before we get into the riding experience, let's talk numbers:

That last number is important. While premium adventure bikes cost $15k-$20k, the NX 500 comes in under $7k. That's not "budget bike" territory—that's "reasonable person" territory.

The parallel-twin engine is shared with the CB500X and Rebel 500. Honda's been refining this engine since 2013. It's proven, reliable, and perfectly adequate. Not exciting, but adequate is underrated.

## On the Road: Where It Actually Excels

### Highway Cruising

Let's start with what most people do most: highway riding. The NX 500 does 120 km/h (75 mph) all day without complaint. The engine spins at around 6,000 rpm at that speed—well below the 8,500 rpm redline. It's not stressed; you're not stressed. Good.

The windscreen is adjustable, which is rare at this price point. In the up position, it deflects wind off your chest. In the down position, it's less effective but looks cooler (priorities, right?). I keep it up because I'm not 22 anymore and I value not having to do push-ups against hurricane winds.

One complaint: wind buffeting around 110-130 km/h. There's a turbulent zone where air hits your helmet just wrong. Tuck your head down an inch, problem solved. Or, you know, slow down. Radical idea.

Fuel economy on the highway? I'm averaging 4.2 L/100km (56 mpg). That 17.5L tank gives you about 400km (250 miles) between fills. For a bike with a 19" front wheel and decent ground clearance, that's impressive.

### City Commuting

This is where the NX 500 surprised me. I bought it for weekend adventures, but it's become my daily commuter. Why? Because it's narrow, light, and has enough low-end torque to dart through traffic without constantly shifting.

The parallel-twin makes peak torque at 6,000 rpm, but there's plenty available from 3,000 rpm up. In the city, I'm usually between 4,000-5,000 rpm in third or fourth gear. It pulls cleanly without lugging. No drama, no vibration, just smooth power delivery.

The upright seating position gives you excellent visibility. You sit high enough to see over cars, which is a massive safety advantage. I've avoided two would-be accidents because I saw brake lights three cars ahead.

Filtering through traffic (where legal) is easy. The bike's width is manageable, the mirrors don't stick out like airplane wings, and the engine doesn't cook your legs. My previous bike (a V-twin cruiser) turned me into a rotisserie chicken in stop-and-go traffic. The NX 500 runs cool.

### Twisty Roads

Here's where opinions diverge. Some reviewers say the NX 500 is "adequate" in the twisties. Those reviewers are cowards.

Is this a supersport? No. Are you going to drag knee? Also no (and if you are, you bought the wrong bike). But the NX 500 handles mountain roads better than it has any right to.

The 19" front wheel—usually a compromise for off-road capability—doesn't feel compromised on pavement. Turn-in is predictable, not vague. The suspension (43mm conventional fork, preload-adjustable rear shock) is soft enough for comfort but doesn't wallow in corners.

I've pushed this bike harder than I probably should on unfamiliar mountain passes. It rewards smooth inputs and punishes ham-fistedness (is that a word? It is now). The front tire (110/80-19) doesn't have the contact patch of a sportbike's 120/70-17, but it's confidence-inspiring once you trust it.

The brakes—single 310mm front disc with ABS—are adequate. Just adequate. They'll stop you without drama, but don't expect race-bike bite. For spirited riding, they're fine. For panic stops, they work. For bragging rights, buy something else.

### Off-Road Capability

Let's be honest: the NX 500 is not a dirt bike. It's a street bike that can handle gravel roads and easy trails. If you're planning hardcore single-track or deep mud, buy a CRF300L Rally.

That said, for fire roads, gravel passes, and "the GPS took me where?" situations, it's shockingly capable.

Ground clearance (185mm) is decent but not generous. The bash plate (standard, bless you Honda) has saved me twice from expensive mistakes. The long-travel suspension (150mm front, 135mm rear) soaks up washboard and ruts without bottoming.

The 21" front wheel would be better for serious off-road, but the 19" is a good compromise. It rolls over rocks and roots without deflecting randomly. The Dunlop Trailmax Mixtour tires are 70/30 (70% road, 30% dirt)—adequate for gravel, sketchy in mud.

I rode 200km of forest service roads in Colorado. The NX 500 handled it without complaint. I was more tired than the bike. Would I do it on a KTM 890? Faster, sure. But I'd also be more terrified and more likely to drop it.

The bike weighs 196kg wet. That's light enough to pick up when (not if) you drop it, heavy enough to feel stable on loose surfaces. I dropped it once in sand. Picked it up myself. No damage except to my ego.

## Design and Ergonomics: The Details Matter

### Riding Position

The bars are wide, slightly swept back, and high. Your hands rest naturally without wrist strain. The seat-to-peg distance is spacious without being cruiser-excessive. The seat itself is firm but comfortable—I've done 6-hour days without needing a sheepskin.

At 5'10" (178cm) with a 30" inseam, I flat-foot both sides. Taller riders will be even happier. Shorter riders might want to look at lowering links or thinner seats. The stock setup is friendly for average-height humans.

The wind protection is better than expected for a naked-ish bike. The screen deflects wind off your chest, but your helmet is in the breeze. Wear earplugs—at 120 km/h, wind noise is fatiguing.

### Storage and Practicality

The bike comes with no luggage. Zero. That's actually refreshing—you're not paying for Honda's overpriced OEM options that you'll replace anyway.

I added SW-Motech TRAX side cases (37L + 45L). They're aluminum, waterproof, and mount solidly. Total cost: $800. Cheaper than Honda's plastic boxes.

The rear rack is standard. It'll hold a tail bag or top case. I bungee a 30L dry bag for weekend trips. Works perfectly.

There's no real "storage" on the bike itself. No glove box, no underseat compartment. The fuel tank is just a fuel tank. Bring a tank bag if you need phone/wallet access.

### Instrumentation

The LCD dash is clear, readable in direct sunlight, and shows everything you need:
- Speedometer (large, easy to read)
- Tachometer (analog-style bar)
- Gear indicator (hallelujah)
- Dual tripmeters
- Fuel gauge (surprisingly accurate)
- Clock
- ABS indicator

No TFT screen, no Bluetooth connectivity, no navigation. Some people will hate this. I love it. Fewer things to break, fewer distractions. My phone goes in my pocket. When I'm riding, I'm riding.

### Build Quality

It's a Honda. The build quality is what you'd expect: excellent.

Paint quality is good. Plastics feel thick, not brittle. Welds are clean. Fasteners are consistent (thank god, no random 8mm/10mm/12mm socket mixtures). The chain is properly lubed from the factory (shocking, I know).

After 5,000km and one drop, the bike looks nearly new. No rattles, no loose bits, no "why is this vibrating?" issues. The only wear is on the pegs and levers—exactly where it should be.

## Features and Technology

### ABS

Dual-channel ABS is standard and non-defeatable. Some riders will bemoan the lack of off-road modes. I get it—I've triggered ABS on gravel and it's disconcerting. But for 90% of riders, non-defeatable ABS is a safety feature that saves lives.

If you're skilled enough that ABS limits you off-road, you probably aren't buying an NX 500. You're buying a 1290 Super Adventure R and cackling maniacally in the desert.

### No Traction Control

There's no traction control. None. In 2024, that feels like an oversight. The CB500X (road-focused sibling) doesn't have it either. Honda's reasoning: the bike makes 47 hp. Traction control is for bikes that might kill you.

Fair point. I've never felt like the bike was spinning up in a way that scared me. Even in rain, the power delivery is manageable.

### Ride Modes

There are no ride modes. You get one mode: "ride." 

Honestly? I don't miss them. Ride modes on most bikes are:
- "Sport" (full power, harsh throttle)
- "Rain" (neutered power, numb throttle)
- "Off-road" (ABS off, wheelies enabled)

The NX 500's single mode is perfectly calibrated for mixed use. The throttle response is linear and predictable. If you want less power, twist less. Revolutionary, I know.

### Suspension Adjustability

The fork is non-adjustable. The rear shock has preload adjustment via a clunky spanner and a rotating collar. Not tool-less, but serviceable.

For solo riding, stock settings are fine. Add luggage and a passenger? Crank the preload up. That's all you need for 95% of use cases.

Serious off-roaders will want fully adjustable suspension. Everyone else won't miss it.

## Comparisons: How Does It Stack Up?

### vs. Yamaha Ténéré 700

The Ténéré is the adventure bike everyone recommends. It makes 72 hp from a 689cc parallel-twin, has better suspension, and looks cooler.

It also costs $10,499—$3,500 more than the NX 500.

Is the Ténéré better? Yes. Is it $3,500 better? For hardcore off-roaders, absolutely. For everyone else? Debatable.

The Ténéré is faster, handles rougher terrain, and has better resale value. The NX 500 is cheaper, easier to ride, and doesn't tempt you to do stupid things (as much).

**Buy the Ténéré if:** You regularly ride challenging off-road terrain and want a bike that can keep up with 450cc dirt bikes.

**Buy the NX 500 if:** You ride 80% pavement, 20% gravel, and don't want to remortgage your house.

### vs. KTM 390 Adventure

The KTM is the "sportier" alternative. It's lighter (172kg), makes similar power (44 hp), and costs $6,799—competitive with the NX 500.

Why would you pick the Honda?

**Reliability.** KTM has a reputation for exciting rides and exciting repair bills. Honda has a reputation for boring rides and never needing repairs. Choose your adventure.

The KTM is more fun to ride hard. The Honda is more pleasant to live with. The KTM makes you feel like a hero. The Honda makes you feel like a responsible adult.

**Buy the KTM if:** You prioritize excitement and don't mind wrenching.

**Buy the NX 500 if:** You want to ride, not wrench. Also, you're over 30.

### vs. Suzuki V-Strom 650

The V-Strom is a legend: bulletproof, practical, boring as hell. It makes more power (71 hp), has a bigger tank (20L), and costs $8,899.

The V-Strom is the better all-rounder if your "all" includes only roads. It's faster, more comfortable at highway speeds, and has decades of aftermarket support.

The NX 500 is better off-road, lighter, and $2,000 cheaper. For mixed-use riding, I'd take the Honda. For touring, I'd take the Suzuki.

**Buy the V-Strom if:** Your "adventure" is highway to hotel.

**Buy the NX 500 if:** Your "adventure" includes at least some dirt.

## The Honest Pros and Cons

### Pros

✅ **Price**: $6,999 is a steal for what you get  
✅ **Fuel economy**: 56 mpg means fewer gas stops  
✅ **Reliability**: It's a Honda. You'll die before the bike does  
✅ **Ergonomics**: Comfortable for average-height riders  
✅ **Low maintenance**: 12,000km service intervals (7,500 miles)  
✅ **Easy to ride**: Friendly for beginners, engaging for intermediates  
✅ **Ground clearance**: Handles gravel roads without drama  
✅ **Weight**: Light enough to manhandle when needed  

### Cons

❌ **Power**: 47 hp is adequate, not exciting  
❌ **No traction control**: Feels like an oversight  
❌ **Wind buffeting**: Turbulent at 110-130 km/h  
❌ **Basic suspension**: Non-adjustable fork, limited damping  
❌ **Small tank**: 17.5L is manageable but not generous  
❌ **Single front disc**: Braking power is adequate, not impressive  
❌ **Limited aftermarket**: Newer model means fewer accessories  
❌ **Resale value**: Unknown (too new to judge)  

## Who Should Buy This Bike?

**Perfect for:**
- First adventure bike buyers who don't want to start on a 1200cc monster
- Commuters who want weekend capability
- Riders on a budget who refuse to compromise on quality
- People who ride 80% pavement, 20% gravel
- Anyone tired of overthinking adventure bikes

**Not for:**
- Hardcore off-roaders who prioritize dirt over pavement
- Highway tourers who want cruise control and heated grips
- Speed freaks who need 100+ hp
- People who need to prove something with their bike choice

## The Verdict

The Honda NX 500 is the adventure bike equivalent of a well-made hammer. It's not sexy. It won't impress your friends (unless your friends have taste). It doesn't have the latest gadgets or Instagram appeal.

But it works. Reliably. Affordably. Without drama.

After 5,000km, I've yet to find a situation where the NX 500 was genuinely inadequate. It's never been too small, too weak, or too limited. It's been... enough. And "enough" is underrated in a world obsessed with "more."

If you're looking for your first adventure bike, or you're downsizing from something bigger, or you just want a practical two-wheeled tool that happens to be fun—this is it. The NX 500 isn't the best adventure bike. It's the one that makes the most sense.

And sometimes, sense is the greatest adventure of all.

**Rating: 8.5/10**

Would I buy it again? Absolutely. Will I keep it when the next shiny thing catches my eye? Probably not. But that's not the bike's fault—that's mine.

## Final Thoughts

I started this review calling the NX 500 "the adventure bike that actually makes sense." After thousands of kilometers, I stand by that. This isn't a bike for posers or Instagram warriors. It's a bike for riders.

It gets you there, brings you back, and doesn't ask for much in between. In an industry obsessed with more—more power, more tech, more price—the NX 500 is refreshingly modest.

Buy one. Ride it. Enjoy it. And when people ask why you didn't get the bigger/faster/cooler bike, smile and say, "Because I wanted to ride, not pay for it."
