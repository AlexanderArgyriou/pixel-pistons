# Honda NX 500: The Adventure Bike That Actually Makes Sense

The Honda NX 500 isn't going to beat a Yamaha Tenere 700 or KTM 890 Adventure in magazine shootouts. It's not trying to. This is Honda's answer to a straightforward question: what if you built an adventure bike for people who actually ride, not just pose?

After 5,000 kilometers across highways, city streets, gravel roads, and some questionable trails, I have opinions.

## First Impressions

The NX 500 doesn't announce itself. No dramatic beak, no aggressive graphics, no luggage that costs more than the bike. It's understated.

My dealer said, "It's not flashy, but you'll appreciate that in six months." He was right. The NX 500 looks like a bike that wants to ride, not park.

The ergonomics are immediately noticeable. The 830mm seat height works for normal humans. The handlebars fall naturally. The pegs don't require unusual flexibility. It feels designed by people who actually ride.

At $6,999, it undercuts premium adventure bikes by $8k-$13k. That's not budget territory—it's reasonable.

The parallel-twin engine comes from the CB500X and Rebel 500. Honda's refined it since 2013. It's proven and adequate. Adequate is underrated.

## On the Road

### Highway

The NX 500 cruises at 120 km/h (75 mph) without strain. The engine spins around 6,000 rpm—well below redline.

The adjustable windscreen is a nice touch at this price. Up position deflects wind off your chest. Down position looks better but works worse. I keep it up.

Wind buffeting happens around 110-130 km/h. There's a turbulent zone where air hits your helmet wrong. Duck your head slightly or slow down.

Fuel economy averages 4.2 L/100km (56 mpg). The 17.5L tank gives about 400km (250 miles) between fills.

### City Commuting

The NX 500 became my daily commuter. It's narrow, light, and has enough low-end torque to move through traffic without constant shifting.

Peak torque hits at 6,000 rpm, but plenty is available from 3,000 rpm up. In the city, I'm usually between 4,000-5,000 rpm in third or fourth. Smooth power, no drama.

The upright position gives excellent visibility. You sit high enough to see over cars, which has helped me avoid accidents. The bike stays cool in stop-and-go traffic, unlike my previous V-twin.

### Twisty Roads

The NX 500 handles better than expected. The 19" front wheel doesn't feel compromised on pavement. Turn-in is predictable. The suspension is soft enough for comfort without wallowing in corners.

It rewards smooth inputs. The brakes (single 310mm front disc) are adequate—they stop you without drama but won't win awards.

### Off-Road

The NX 500 isn't a dirt bike. It's a street bike that handles gravel roads and easy trails. For hardcore single-track, buy a CRF300L Rally.

For fire roads and gravel passes, it's capable. Ground clearance (185mm) is decent. The standard bash plate has saved me twice. The suspension (150mm front, 135mm rear) handles washboard without bottoming.

I rode 200km of Colorado forest service roads without issue. At 196kg wet, it's light enough to pick up when dropped, heavy enough to feel stable on loose surfaces. I dropped it once in sand, picked it up myself, no damage except to my ego.

## Design and Ergonomics

### Riding Position

Wide, swept-back handlebars. Spacious seat-to-peg distance. Firm but comfortable seat—I've done 6-hour days comfortably.

At 5'10" (178cm) with a 30" inseam, I flat-foot both sides. The wind protection is better than expected. Your helmet is in the breeze, so wear earplugs at highway speeds.

### Storage

No luggage included. I added SW-Motech TRAX side cases ($800). The standard rear rack holds a tail bag or top case.

No onboard storage—no glove box, no underseat compartment. Bring a tank bag for phone/wallet access.

### Instrumentation

Clear LCD dash with speedometer, tachometer, gear indicator, dual tripmeters, fuel gauge, clock, and ABS indicator. No TFT screen, no Bluetooth, no navigation. I prefer it this way—fewer distractions, fewer things to break.

### Build Quality

It's a Honda. Paint is good, plastics feel thick, welds are clean. After 5,000km and one drop, the bike looks nearly new. No rattles, no loose bits.

## Features

Dual-channel ABS is standard and non-defeatable. Some riders want off-road modes, but for 90% of riders, ABS saves lives.

No traction control. The bike makes 47 hp—Honda's reasoning is that traction control is for bikes that might kill you. Fair point. I've never felt the power delivery was unmanageable.

No ride modes. You get one: ride. The throttle response is linear and predictable. I don't miss multiple modes.

The fork is non-adjustable. The rear shock has preload adjustment. For solo riding, stock settings work. Add luggage or a passenger, increase preload. That's sufficient for most riders.

## Comparisons

### vs. Yamaha Ténéré 700

The Ténéré makes 72 hp, has better suspension, and costs $10,499—$3,500 more.

Is it better? Yes. Is it $3,500 better? For hardcore off-roaders, absolutely. For everyone else, debatable.

**Ténéré if:** You regularly ride challenging terrain.  
**NX 500 if:** You ride 80% pavement, 20% gravel.

### vs. KTM 390 Adventure

The KTM is lighter (172kg), makes similar power (44 hp), and costs $6,799.

Why pick the Honda? **Reliability.** KTM has a reputation for exciting rides and exciting repair bills. Honda has a reputation for boring rides and never needing repairs.

**KTM if:** You prioritize excitement.  
**NX 500 if:** You want to ride, not wrench.

### vs. Suzuki V-Strom 650

The V-Strom makes more power (71 hp), has a bigger tank (20L), and costs $8,899.

The V-Strom is better for roads only—faster, more comfortable at highway speeds. The NX 500 is better off-road, lighter, and $2,000 cheaper.

**V-Strom if:** Your adventure is highway to hotel.  
**NX 500 if:** Your adventure includes dirt.

## The Honest Pros and Cons

**Pros:**  
✅ Price ($6,999)  
✅ Fuel economy (56 mpg)  
✅ Honda reliability  
✅ Comfortable ergonomics  
✅ Low maintenance (12,000km service intervals)  
✅ Easy to ride  
✅ Light enough to manage  

**Cons:**  
❌ 47 hp is adequate, not exciting  
❌ No traction control  
❌ Wind buffeting at 110-130 km/h  
❌ Basic suspension  
❌ Small tank (17.5L)  
❌ Single front disc  
❌ Limited aftermarket support  

## Who Should Buy This

**Perfect for:** First adventure bike buyers, commuters wanting weekend capability, riders on a budget who won't compromise on quality, anyone riding 80% pavement/20% gravel.

**Not for:** Hardcore off-roaders, highway tourers wanting cruise control, speed enthusiasts, people who need to prove something with their bike.

## The Verdict

The Honda NX 500 is the adventure bike equivalent of a well-made hammer. Not sexy, won't impress your friends, doesn't have the latest gadgets.

But it works. Reliably. Affordably. Without drama.

After 5,000km, I've yet to find a situation where the NX 500 was genuinely inadequate. It's been enough. And "enough" is underrated in a world obsessed with "more."

If you're looking for your first adventure bike, downsizing from something bigger, or just want a practical tool that's also fun—this is it. The NX 500 isn't the best adventure bike. It's the one that makes the most sense.

**Rating: 8.5/10**

Would I buy it again? Absolutely.
